Methodology 方法

From source to substantiated brief.

A Japanese-first monitoring project for digital-assets regulation and institutional market infrastructure.

On this page

01 · Observe

Start with Japanese originals.

The configured sources cover Japanese releases from the Financial Services Agency and Japan Exchange Group, alongside Progmat news, articles and working-group reports. This is a focused source set, not comprehensive coverage of every Japanese regulator or market participant.

Discovery and retrieval preserve source URLs and document revisions. When a listing points to a primary document, the retrieval chain records that relationship.

Regulator statements, exchange announcements and company publications carry different authority. A company’s pilot or a working group’s proposal should not be read as an enacted rule.

02 · Ground

Make the evidence inspectable.

Source text is extracted with page or heading locations. Relevant documents are inventoried for material changes, actors, instruments, amounts and dates before an English brief is composed.

Model-generated claims are checked against verbatim source excerpts. A separate entailment stage checks whether the cited excerpt supports each claim. Publication validation covers the headline, significance, affected parties and next steps as well as the summary. The verifier can use the same model as the generator, so a separate call is not independent proof of correctness. Automated checks help expose errors; they do not substitute for editorial judgment.

Published briefs include their Japanese source link and claim-to-evidence mappings. The original document remains the reference when the English interpretation is uncertain.

Excerpts keep every character of the source. Line breaks from the original PDF are joined between Japanese characters, as the CSS text specification asks browsers to do, so they do not show as stray spaces.

03 · Review

Publication is a human decision.

Drafts require explicit human approval before they can enter this archive. Review examines the source evidence, completeness of material changes, exact dates and the distinction between an announcement, proposal, pilot and operative requirement.

Model evaluation currently identifies factual-completeness gaps. Passing a citation check alone is not sufficient: a brief can cite everything it says while still omitting an important part of the source.

The public site is an export of approved records. Pending and rejected drafts, reviewer notes and internal model logs are excluded. Updates appear when a new publication export is deployed.

04 · Interpret

Read with the boundaries in view.

This is an independent research project. It is not affiliated with the organisations it monitors and does not provide legal advice. Coverage is selective and publication is not real time.

Source material may change after retrieval. Follow the original link to check for later revisions or related notices. Source listing and editorial review dates are labelled separately; neither necessarily represents an effective date. A listing can link to an older document. Where that happens, the brief must distinguish the document date from the listing date and preserve the source’s original tense. A planned event does not become a confirmed event simply because its target date has passed. The publication manifest records which approved briefs were included in each export. A withdrawal or correction reaches this site when the replacement export is deployed.

To suggest a correction, open a public issue with the page URL, the disputed wording and the relevant Japanese source passage. Corrections are reviewed against the original evidence before a replacement brief is published.

An empty archive means no briefs have been approved and published. It does not mean that there were no relevant developments.
